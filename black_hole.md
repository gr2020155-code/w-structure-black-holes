# Black Holes in the W-Structure

**Author:** Hrachya Danielyan. **Version:** 1.1, November 2026. **License:** CC BY 4.0. Russian original: [black_hole_ru.md](black_hole_ru.md).

In the W-Structure, a black hole is the limit 3W: all possible links are realized, and nothing changes inside. There is no singularity: inside the limit there is no three-dimensional space. Outside, space densifies layer by layer, and the field has the form e^(±δ). With δ = GM/(rc²) this is the exponential metric, the same as in Papapetrou (1954) and Yilmaz (1958). Hence the difference from general relativity (GR): the throat is at 2.72 GM/c², and the shadow is 4.6 % larger (for the unmodified exponential metric, section 4). What is new here is the derivation of the field form from layers and links, and what lies beyond the throat; the numbers for this metric are known.

## 3. What a black hole is

**3W is saturation** [author, O3]. All possible links are realized. Every attempt goes along an already existing link and fails. Inside there are no changes; the boundary grows by what arrives from outside (position 1).

**There is no singularity** [derived]. A singularity here is a place where quantities become infinite. There is no such place:

- inside the limit there is no three-dimensional space (position 11) — so there is no point where quantities could go to infinity;
- outside, the rate e^(−δ) is finite; at the throat δ = 1 (section 4), and the rate is e^(−1) ≈ 0.37 of the distant one;
- one discrete step remains to the limit (position 2).

Paths of bodies end at the throat: what reaches it passes into indistinguishability and increases the throat (position 12). This is not a break in the description but a transition into 3W.

**There is no event horizon in the sense of GR.** In GR the rate drops to zero at the horizon. Here the rate at the throat is e^(−1): there is no surface of infinite slowing. Below, "throat layer" is used instead of "horizon".

**What lies beyond 3W** [compendium F13; open]. The transition is 3W = 40 (the next dimensionality) or 3W = 00. The choice depends on whether it is proved that three dimensions are the limit of W for dimensionality.

## 4. Space around

**Rate** [derived from O5–O6; G2]. The rate is defined only relative to the neighbouring layer, so the rate ratios of neighbouring layers multiply. If the logarithm of the ratio of neighbouring layers equals the increment of δ, and the step between layers is small compared with the scale on which δ changes, the product gives e^(−δ) relative to a distant observer.

**Densification of path** [derived from positions 7–8]. The number of links per unit path changes from layer to layer by a factor of the same size, inverse to the rate factor, so there are e^(+δ) steps per unit r. This is the same phenomenon as the slowing of time. The factor is the same along the radius and around the circle [condition: isotropic densification]; without densification the circumference of layer r is 2πr.

**The magnitude of δ** [condition]. δ = GM/(rc²), where r is the layer count (position 9). Checks of this law:

- the perihelion of Mercury is a second-order check in GM/(rc²). Both factors are needed: the second-order term in the rate and the first-order path factor e^(+δ). With e^(±δ) the result is the same as in GR — about 43″ per century; the observed excess agrees. The rate alone, without the path factor, would give only a third, about 14.3″;
- light deflection at the Sun with e^(±δ) is 1.75″, as in G1. The "two channels" of G1 are exactly the rate and the path factor e^(+δ), not an addition to them; otherwise the contribution would be counted twice.

Near the throat, where δ ~ 1, the law is not tested (section 9). The circular photon orbit lies at δ = 1/2, inside this untested region. The perihelion and light deflection fix the law only up to second order in δ. A third-order change leaves them unchanged but shifts the shadow: for example, a rate e^(−δ+εδ³) changes the critical impact parameter by −ε/8 in relative terms. So the shadow numbers below are predictions of the unmodified exponential metric, not of every law with the same weak field.

**Where the mass is and under which condition the numbers hold** [condition]. The numbers below are obtained for δ = GM/(rc²), that is, as if all of the mass M lies deeper than the layer considered. By position 6 the mass lies in the layers around, denser closer to the limit. So the numbers hold outside the massive layers. If the massive layers are thin compared with GM/c², the numbers hold approximately near the throat as well. The law of mass distribution over the layers is not derived (section 9).

**Throat** [calculated under a condition]. The circumference of layer r is 2π·r·e^(GM/(rc²)). It is smallest at r = GM/c² (δ = 1); its circumferential radius is e·GM/c² ≈ 2.72 GM/c².

**Observable numbers** [calculated under a condition]. Radii are circumference divided by 2π, in units of GM/c².

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Throat (GR: horizon) | e ≈ 2.72 | 2 | — (not observed directly) |
| Circular photon orbit | 2√e ≈ 3.30 | 3 | +9.9 % |
| Shadow radius (critical impact parameter) | 2e ≈ 5.44 | 3√3 ≈ 5.20 | +4.6 % |

Thin bright subrings in the image converge to the critical curve of diameter 4e ≈ 10.87 (GR 6√3 ≈ 10.39). This is the same quantity as the shadow — one check, but independent of the model of the emitting gas.

**Relation to known results** [check]. A field with rate e^(−δ) and path factor e^(+δ) at δ = GM/(rc²) is the exponential metric of Papapetrou (1954) and Yilmaz (1958). The same numbers have been obtained for it in the literature: a throat with circumferential radius e·GM/c², a circular photon orbit 2√e·GM/c², ISCO ≈ 6.338 GM/c²; the shadow radius 2e·GM/c² follows from the same work (Boonserm, Ngampitipan, Simpson, Visser, 2018). The agreement is a check of the calculation, not a priority claim.

What is our own here:

- the derivation of the field form: rate and link density change from layer to layer by one factor (positions 7–8), r is the layer count (position 9);
- what lies beyond the throat. In the literature the throat of the exponential metric is a traversable wormhole into another region of space. In the Structure there is no three-dimensional space beyond the throat: there is 3W, and what reaches it does not return (positions 3, 12, 13).

**Growth** [derived]. The throat e·GM/c² is proportional to the mass. By position 12 everything that reaches the throat increases it; by O4 the limit only grows.

## 5. Rotation

**What rotates** [author, position 5]. The black hole itself (3W) does not rotate. The layer W − 1 rotates; the limit W itself only grows. Matter in the outer layers orbits at different speeds; this is a property of any gravity and by itself does not distinguish the Structure from GR.

**Why there is rotation** [author, position 15; O3]. A black hole forms from a rotating star. Angular momentum is conserved in contraction (O3), so the layers spin up: the smaller the radius, the faster the revolution. In the layer W − 1 matter is held back — one step remains to the limit, and the inflow is one-dimensional — and orbits before merging with "nothing". Strictly zero rotation would require a strictly non-rotating star; real stars are never like that.

**Speed limit** [derived from position 10 and O6]. One indivisible change takes one indivisible interval of local time. Hence the layer W − 1 does not move faster than light in local time.

**The bound χ ≤ 1 is not derived from the Structure** [derived]. χ = Jc/(GM²), where J is the total angular momentum of the layers (3W itself does not rotate, position 5) and M is the mass seen from outside; it is not a speed.

- In GR χ ≤ 1 because at χ > 1 the horizon disappears and a singularity is exposed. For an extremal rotating GR black hole the equatorial circumferential radius of the horizon is 2GM/c².
- In the Structure there is no singularity, and the throat is larger: e·GM/c² versus 2GM/c², e/2 ≈ 1.36 times.
- The GR argument for χ ≤ 1 relies on a singularity that does not exist here. The Structure's own limit on χ is not derived: a simple Newtonian estimate (a ring at the throat moving at the speed of light) proves nothing — for an extremal Kerr black hole it would give χ = 2 instead of the true 1.

**Rotation of the layers changes the field** [author, position 17]. Motion of the source mass affects the field: the compendium calculates the frame dragging of the Gravity Probe B gyroscopes this way (G4; relies on G1 [proposal] and G2). This is not the same motion channel of G1 as in section 4: there the light moves, not the source; how G4 is obtained from G1 is not given in the compendium. Hence [derived under a condition]: rotating layers drag their surroundings, and the field at the throat, and with it the shadow and the ISCO, may depend on the rotation of the layers. The law of this influence near the throat is not derived; the ceiling on χ is not computed (section 9).

## 6. What is seen from outside

**What reaches the throat does not return** [derived from positions 3, 4, 12]. Matter, light and waves that reach the throat pass into the indistinguishability of 3W and increase the throat. Hence:

- **the throat itself neither shines nor reflects**: rays with impact parameter less than 2e·GM/c² reach the throat and do not return; inside the shadow only the light of gas lying on the line of sight in front of the throat is seen; light from gas at the throat itself is strongly dimmed;
- **there are no echoes**: an echo in merger signals would arise from waves reflecting off a surface, and there is nothing to reflect;
- **there is no surface glow**: there is nothing to heat up; the energy of infalling matter passes into indistinguishability.

**Last light** [derived under the condition of section 4]. There is no event horizon, so an infalling body reaches the throat in a finite time of the distant observer. Light emitted at the throat itself arrives with a finite redshift: 1 + z = e ≈ 2.72 for a static emitter; for matter freely falling from rest at infinity, from ≈ 12 to ≈ 14 depending on the ray direction (≈ 14 for a ray strictly outward; rays deviating from the radius by more than ≈ 47° are captured); dimming 2·10⁴–4·10⁴. In GR the redshift at the horizon is infinite. This difference is observable in principle; the brightness inside the shadow has to be calculated (section 9).

**No steady Hawking flux** [derived under a condition]. The thermal Hawking flux does not need matter inside; it arises in the quantum fields outside, and it requires an exponential stretching of light rays — a redshift that grows without bound (a horizon is not required, but this stretching is; Barceló, Liberati, Sonego, Visser, 2011). The exponential metric has no horizon, and the redshift at the throat is finite (section 6, "Last light"). Therefore the standard mechanism gives no steady thermal flux. This does not say that Hawking's calculation is wrong; it says that it does not apply to this throat. Open: a transient burst while the geometry changes during formation, and the effect of the throat as a perfectly absorbing boundary on quantum fields (section 9). Separately, by position 11 nothing radiates from 3W itself [author].

## 7. Masses of black holes

**There is one primordial black hole** [author, position 14]. It is the black hole at the zero point O. All observed black holes are not primordial: away from O, only collapse leads to 3W.

**No black holes lighter than the neutron-star limit minus the mass carried away** [derived under a condition, positions 14, 16]. Collapse reaches 3W only when a neutron star cannot hold the matter. Hence the mass of any observed black hole is not less than the limit of a non-rotating neutron star minus the mass carried away in collapse. (Rotation raises the limit for the star itself by about 20 %.) The value of the limit is not computed in the Structure. The heaviest measured neutron stars are about 2.1 and 2.35 solar masses (PSR J0740+6620 and PSR J0952−0607).

## 9. Open questions

1. **3W = 40 or 00.** Depends on a proof that three dimensions are the limit of W for dimensionality (F13).
2. **Mass distribution over the layers and δ(r).** The numbers of section 4 hold outside the massive layers. A law of mass distribution over the layers (position 6) and δ(r) inside the massive layers are needed. Related: which links count as mass. If, following O8, the densification of links e^(+δ) around the black hole itself counted as mass, its total mass would grow without bound with distance; so something else must count as mass (for example, its change from layer to layer). The definitions of mass are not reduced to one.
3. **The law of δ near the throat.** It is checked against the perihelion to second order. At δ ~ 1 it is not derived from the structure. The shadow depends on it: the photon orbit lies at δ = 1/2 (section 4).
4. **Does the layer W − 1 radiate?** Position 11 closes 3W. The layer W − 1 is not saturated and rotates; there are changes in it. A reason why it does not radiate is needed.
5. **The field of rotating layers.** A law is needed by which the rotation of the layers (frame dragging, G4) changes the field at the throat. The size and shape of the shadow, the ISCO, the ceiling on χ and the difference from GR for rotating systems depend on it.
6. **Field equations and merger signals.** Without them there is no prediction of the ringdown after a merger. In the Structure waves do not return from the throat; the spectrum of these oscillations has to be calculated.
7. **Brightness inside the shadow.** A calculation of the emission of gas on rays inside the shadow is needed, including between the photon orbit and the throat, taking the motion of the gas into account.
8. **The maximum neutron-star mass in the Structure.** At present the bound is taken from observations.
9. **How the external mass M grows.** There is no mass in the core (position 6), and what reaches the throat becomes indistinguishability and increases the throat (position 12). How the mass M seen from outside grows, and how it relates to the mass of the layers, is not derived.
10. **The black hole at the point O and the edge of space** (F5, F11). Not considered in this file.
11. **The shadow for rotating layers.** The layers of real black holes rotate (position 15: angular momentum of the progenitor star). The numbers of section 4 and the shadow 2e are given for non-rotating layers — the limit of slow rotation. A law is needed by which the rotation of the layers changes the size and shape of the shadow (see 9.5); until then the comparison with observations is fair only for black holes with slowly rotating layers.
12. **The path of collapse (condition 16).** Why, away from O, there is no path to 3W that bypasses a neutron star (for example, early densifications). How much mass can be carried away in collapse: without this bound the threshold of section 8 is not defined.
13. **Entropy and area.** Inside 3W there are no distinctions (position 11). Therefore everything by which one black hole differs from another lies on the throat, and the number of its states is set by the area of the throat, not by its volume. This agrees with the Bekenstein–Hawking law (entropy ∝ area). Not derived: (a) the link "number of throat points → number of states"; (b) the numerical factor (in the standard formula, 1/4 in Planck units): the step length is not defined in the Structure, so the numbers cannot be compared.
14. **Emission during formation and the absorbing boundary.** The steady Hawking flux is absent (section 6). Not calculated: a transient burst while the geometry changes during collapse, and what the throat, as a boundary that absorbs everything, does to quantum fields.
